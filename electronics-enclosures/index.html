From PCB to Printed Enclosure

Hardware startups in Kathmandu often need an enclosure fast — for a demo, an investor meeting, or a first field test. We 3D print enclosures around your PCB and connector layout in PLA or PETG, iterating quickly if the first fit needs adjustment.

Repair shops also come to us for one-off replacement housings, brackets, and mounts for equipment where the original part is no longer available — send us a photo and rough measurements and we'll tell you if it's printable.

Because this is prototyping work, we expect a few rounds of fit-checking on tight-tolerance jobs. We'll flag upfront if a design needs a second iteration rather than surprise you with a redo later.

What We Need From You

  • PCB or component dimensions, and connector/port positions
  • An STL/STEP file if you already have a design
  • Expected operating environment (heat, handling, outdoor use)
  • Quantity — single prototype or small batch

FAQ

Can you design an enclosure from scratch, or do I need a 3D file?
Send us your PCB dimensions, connector positions, and any mounting requirements, and we can discuss designing the enclosure. If you already have an STL or STEP file, we can print directly from it.
Which material is best for an electronics enclosure?
PETG is a common choice for enclosures thanks to its durability and heat resistance; PLA works well for early prototypes and low-heat applications. We'll recommend a material once we know how the enclosure will be used.
Can you print a small batch, not just one unit?
Yes, we print both one-off prototypes and small batches for startups moving toward a first production run.
