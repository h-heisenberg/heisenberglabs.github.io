3D Printing in Nepal: A Guide

How 3D printing works, when to use it, and how to get a quote from Heisenberg Labs in Kathmandu.

3D printing, or additive manufacturing, builds objects layer by layer from a digital design instead of cutting or molding material away. In Kathmandu, that makes it a practical option for architectural models, electronics enclosures, custom accessories, and personalized gifts — jobs where a one-off or small batch would be slow or expensive to produce any other way.

Where 3D Printing Fits in Nepal

  • Architectural scale models for client presentations and approvals
  • Electronics enclosures and hardware prototypes for startups
  • Non-safety-critical bike and car accessories
  • Personalized gifts — figurines, lithophane lamps, keychains, trophies
  • Teaching aids and hands-on learning for schools and colleges

FDM vs Resin

FDM (Filament)

Uses PLA or PETG filament. Good for functional, durable parts and larger objects; cost-effective and fast for everyday use.

Resin

Liquid photopolymer cured by UV light. Produces high detail and a smooth surface, best for small, detailed pieces.

Materials at a Glance

MaterialBest Use
PLAPrototypes, models, gifts — easy to print, good detail
PETGDurable, outdoor-friendly parts — enclosures, accessories

FAQ

FDM vs resin — what should I choose?
FDM (PLA/PETG) suits durable, functional parts and larger objects; resin suits small parts needing high detail and a smooth finish.
What file formats should I send?
STL is the standard format; OBJ also works. If you only have a 2D drawing or CAD file in another format, mention it when you contact us.
Do you deliver outside Kathmandu?
Yes — pickup is available in Kalanki, Kathmandu, and delivery can be arranged across Lalitpur, Bhaktapur, and the rest of Nepal.
